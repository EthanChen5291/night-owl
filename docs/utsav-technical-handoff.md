# Utsav's technical contribution to Barn Owl

## Devpost-ready summary

Barn Owl is a prototype for finding city blocks that reports may miss and planning where camera nodes should go. I worked on the link between its city risk map and a Raspberry Pi camera detector. I completed the React/three.js demo so it can show H3 risk cells, suggested sensor sites, inspection backtests, and incoming sightings even when the optional 3D city data is absent. I helped make the API the source of truth for how an accepted sighting changes a cell's probability and the order of suggested sites. For the camera side, I built and evaluated a two-class YOLO11n detector for a **dark plush rat in lit scenes**, using reviewed camera footage, grayscale training, and reproducible Modal GPU runs. The locked v4 model scored **0.926 rat AP50 on 59 reserved same-session frames** but fell to **0.883 on 439 reviewed new-room frames**, below the 0.90 target. V5 adapted to that failure and scored **0.975 rat AP50 on 146 development frames**. Those frames include footage used to build and select V5; no fresh post-V5 test has measured generalization. Positive live-camera detection and timed event recall remain open.

## What I built and connected

- **Map and planning UI.** The [web app](../web/README.md) keeps Ethan's three.js city view, adds a usable H3 and plan-pin view without the optional city bake, and exposes month-aware cells, a 5/10/20-site budget, cell details, and the backtest. It labels model, fixture, synthetic, and stale states so a fallback cannot be mistaken for fresh evidence. Events arrive through Server-Sent Events, with polling and reconnection when a stream closes. The browser refetches cells and plans after live events; it does not calculate a competing posterior. [Implementation](../web/src/App.tsx) · [API client](../web/src/api.ts)
- **Evidence loop.** The [FastAPI hub](../api/README.md) accepts the Pi event schema, persists the event queue, and maintains a Beta-Binomial posterior for each H3 cell and month. Accepted rat detections update the served cell's risk and can re-rank existing tree-backed plan candidates; rejected or duplicate events do not change the posterior. The API reports the actual source month and whether data came from a model export or a fixture. The standard demo cell is outside the September planner candidate pool, so a sighting there changes the cell without pretending a plan pin moved. [Routes](../api/main.py) · [State and overlays](../api/store.py)
- **Recorded integration.** A saved-video **v2** replay produced an accepted event through the local API and updated the map and a plan candidate. This verifies the recorded-footage path, separate from a live v4 camera run. [Delivery evidence](../plan/utsav-delivery.md) · [Replay procedure](../vision/RUNBOOK.md)

## How the detector was made

The target was the team's plush prop, not a wild rat. Reviewers drew YOLO boxes around the visible plush body and visible people, checked frames against their source clips, recorded explicit empty frames, and excluded uncertain examples. The v4 training set has **572 reviewed frames, 326 plush boxes, 290 person boxes, and 165 frames with neither class**. It includes hard negatives from the scene, such as the stationary chair and other dark objects. After v3 scored well on repeatedly used validation clips but fired on chair fabric in a separate new-angle test, we corrected 36 overly broad person labels and added that now-consumed positive and empty footage to v4 training. Those v3 test clips are development data for v4, never a fresh v4 test. Infrared clips were inspected but excluded because the plush could not be identified reliably for labeling. [Dataset provenance](../vision/TRAINING_RESULTS.md) · [Recording and review rules](../vision/RECORDING.md)

V4 starts from pretrained YOLO11n and uses two classes, plush rat and person. Frames are converted to grayscale and copied across three channels to match Pi inference. A bounded Modal L4 run trained at 416 pixels for 150 epochs with AdamW, batch 8, and recorded augmentations. We selected the checkpoint with the highest rat AP50 while keeping person AP50 at least 0.84, then exported static-square ONNX. A separate CPU verifier used the same square-416 protocol for PyTorch and ONNX; their AP50 scores matched. This matters because an earlier apparent conversion gap came from comparing rectangular PyTorch validation with square ONNX validation. The candidate model, dataset fingerprints, checkpoint selection, package versions, and reports are preserved in the [v4 bundle](../vision/artifacts/README.md). [Training code](../vision/modal_train.py) · [Trial log](../vision/HILL_CLIMB.md) · [Modal instructions](../vision/MODAL.md)

V5 began from the exact selected V4 checkpoint after V4 missed the parked plush and fired on a black case in the changed room. Its frozen data contain **652 training frames and 146 development validation frames**. The three V4 new-room recordings were deliberately consumed for V5 development; their V5 scores cannot serve as a new independent test. One bounded Modal L4 run trained for 60 epochs. We selected epoch 35 using the minimum rat AP50 across three whole development clips before replay, then checked PyTorch/ONNX parity and retained the 0.70 runtime confidence. Ethan is working on fresh clips for independent testing; an unidentified 92-second recording was excluded. [V5 recipe, provenance, and results](../vision/V5_RESULTS.md) · [Candidate bundle](../vision/artifacts/rat-litroom-v5-development-candidate-20260927.zip)

## What the measurements support

| Check | Rat AP50 | Person AP50 | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 49-frame development validation, reused for recipe and checkpoint selection | 0.9583 | 0.8631 | Tuned validation evidence |
| 59-frame, four-clip reserve, evaluated once after model and runtime settings were locked | 0.9258 | 0.8764 | Same-session, same-camera, same-table temporal holdout |
| 439 reviewed frames from three post-lock new-room recordings | 0.8827 | 0.3439 | Changed room; rat AP50 below the 0.90 target and person performance fell sharply |
| V5 on 146 development frames, including the now-consumed moving new-room clip | 0.9753 | 0.7989 | Tuned development evidence; weakest whole clip rat AP50 0.9608, fresh test pending |

On development video, confidence 0.50 produced six false events on a stationary object. We chose confidence **0.70 on development footage before opening the reserve**. Full replay of the four same-session reserve videos then emitted 20 events. Reviewers saw the plush in all 20 saved frame-and-crop pairs and found no other-object event in that set. The 20 events are repeated detector firings, **not 20 independently timed pushes**; they cannot establish push recall or a general false-alert rate. AP50 measures ranked box detections, not real-world accuracy. [Development and reserve results](../vision/HILL_CLIMB.md) · [Original v4 bundle](../vision/artifacts/README.md)

The later new-room test exposed a runtime gap. At the locked 0.70 confidence threshold, 15 of 31 moving-plush ground-truth boxes matched; replay produced nine plush events across four appearances and one false event on a black case. The first segment of the fourth appearance was missed until the plush reappeared. A parked plush had 137 ground-truth boxes but produced no 0.70-confidence proposal or event, despite 0.994 AP at lower confidence. The 181.21-second negative recording yielded zero events across all 2,718 replayed frames. These are recorded-video results and do not verify positive detection from the live Pi camera. The separate [new-room evaluation supplement](../vision/artifacts/rat-litroom-v4-new-room-evaluation-20260927.zip) is packaged and passed CRC and all 1,918 listed file hashes; the original v4 bundle predates these results and is unchanged.

At the unchanged 0.70 threshold, V5's five full development-video replays produced 14 plush events on the moving new-room clip, 64 on the parked plush, 64 on the old metal clip, 44 on the old floor clip, and zero on the 181-second people-only clip. Reviewers checked every emitted crop against its source frame and found no other-object event in these five clips. The parked clip represents one continuous exposure, not 64 pushes. All five recordings influenced V5 development, so this verifies behavior on known footage without estimating fresh-scene precision or timed-push recall. [Replay audit and limits](../vision/V5_RESULTS.md)

The exact v4 ONNX model ran on a **saved frame on the physical Pi** with median inference time of **56.32 ms**. The optional frame bridge and isolated worker passed offline checks. After explicit approval, the bridge was enabled through one service restart for a local-only live-worker trial. The worker processed **2,052 frames at a mean 57.67 ms per frame** and made zero rat proposals; no plush was presented, so this was a throughput check, not a detection test. The worker was stopped after the trial while the camera feed continued. The trial did not post events to the API. V5 also ran on the physical Pi using saved frames: one plush proposal scored 0.931, one negative frame had none, and median inference was about 60 ms. Neither check verifies positive live-camera detection, API posting, or 20 timed pushes. [Pi integration notes](../vision/pi/LIVE_BRIDGE.md) · [Promotion gates](../vision/RUNBOOK.md)
